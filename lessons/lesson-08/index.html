Internet Research & Information Management

Output: Client Research Brief

Step 1 of 9

Learn

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Translate a client's question into a research task.
  • Find and evaluate useful sources.
  • Cross-check important information.
  • Separate facts from assumptions.
  • Present findings in a decision-ready format.
Key idea

Research is not simply searching Google. The output should help the client understand something, compare options, or make a next decision.

Learn

Research workflow

QUESTION→ SEARCH→ VERIFY→ ORGANIZE→ SUMMARIZE→ CITE
Learn

Source quality

  • Prefer primary sources when the question concerns a company's own product, policy, documentation, or official data.
  • Use reputable secondary sources for context and independent comparison.
  • Check dates when information can change.
  • Cross-check important claims.
  • Record the source for each meaningful factual claim.
Learn

Decision-ready research

Do not overwhelm the client with copied information. Organize findings around the decision criteria.

Compare the options

Research three project-management tools suitable for a five-person remote business. For each, record the price exactly as the source shows it (or "not published" — don't estimate), key features, pros and cons, where you found it, and the date you checked.

Practice → Decide

Conflicting prices

One source (dated this month) lists a tool at $12/user/month. Another source (dated two years ago) lists it at $8/user/month. The client wants the current price.

What should you do?

Practice

Fact, assumption, or needs checking?

You're preparing the project-tool research brief. Classify each statement from your notes.

  • Fact (sourced): a source you checked states it. In your brief, record where it came from (the page or document name, or a link) and the date you checked it, for example "Vendor pricing page, checked 11 Nov 2026". If you can't record both, treat it as Needs verification.
  • Assumption: your own expectation or estimate; no source states it. Label it as an assumption in the brief.
  • Needs verification: someone claims it, but the source is old, unofficial or secondhand. Check it against the original source, then record that source and the date.
