Professional Communication & Client Updates

Output: Client Communication Pack

Step 1 of 8

Learn

Before you start

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Write clear, concise professional messages.
  • Ask effective clarification questions.
  • Give useful progress updates.
  • Communicate delays and mistakes early.
  • Know when to escalate.
Key idea

Good communication reduces uncertainty. The client should know what is happening, what you need, and what happens next.

Learn

Clear before clever

Professional communication does not require complicated English. Aim for clarity, conciseness, respect, specificity, and appropriate timing.

The four-part client update
PartQuestion it answers
ContextWhat task or issue are you referring to?
StatusWhat is happening now?
ActionWhat did you do or what will you do?
Next stepWhat happens next?
"Hi Mark, quick update on the vendor research. I reviewed eight options and narrowed them to three that meet the budget requirement. I'm comparing contract terms now and will add the final comparison to the tracker this afternoon."
Learn

Asking for clarification

what you understand + what is unclear + the specific question
"I understand that you'd like the spreadsheet cleaned and standardized. For duplicate records, would you prefer that I remove them or flag them for your review?"
Learn

Reporting a delay, reporting a mistake

Reporting a delay: Do not wait until a deadline has passed. Explain the issue, the likely impact, and a practical option.

"Hi Anna, I wanted to flag a potential delay with the contact cleanup. There are significantly more duplicate records than expected, so I may need until 3 PM tomorrow rather than the original noon target. I can prioritize the urgent records first if you'd like."

Reporting a mistake:

what happened → what you did → what remains → what you recommend
"I noticed that I uploaded the previous version of the report. I've replaced it with the corrected version and checked the file. No client-facing information was sent from the incorrect version."

Do not hide errors or create unnecessary panic.

Learn

When to escalate

  • The decision exceeds your authority.
  • Instructions conflict.
  • The task involves significant risk.
  • Confidential information may be exposed.
  • A deadline may be missed.
  • The client's decision or approval is required.
Practice

Write the message

Your client asks you to schedule a meeting with John, but there are two contacts named John.
Suggested answer: "I have two contacts listed as John. Could you confirm which one you'd like me to schedule with?"
